lecture 2 summary 

Sampling = taking measurements.
Fourier transform = seeing frequencies.
Convolution = combining/filtering signals.
Reconstruction = rebuilding the continuous signal.
Aliasing = sampling too slowly.



Think of recoding music
* A microphone produces a continues electrical signal 
	* x(t)
* We measure it many times per second
	* x(n) = x(nT_s)
* With
	* fs = 1 / T_s



Sampling creates repeated spectra in frequency 
	* X(f) -> Xs(f)


If 
	* f-s > 2*f_max
	* the copies do not overlap, so theoretically we can reconstruct the original signal



if 
	* f-s < 2*f_max
	* they overlap
		* (Its alaising) ... and information is lost
	

(Those are the central ideas of Lecture 2) 

Fourier transform lets us switch between 
* time/space domain -> frequency domain 

while convolution and multiplication are connected:
convolution in time -> multiplication in frequency 
multiplication in time -> convolution in frequency
