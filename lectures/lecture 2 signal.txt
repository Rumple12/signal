(Deterministic vs stochastic signals)
A deterministic signal is completely predictable... like a sin frequncy equation
if we know f, fs, or phi ... then we know exactly what every future sample will be.
It is mathematically easy to analyze, but real-world signals are rarely this perfect.

(Stochastic signal)
A stochastic signal contains randomness.
z(n)=y(n)+w(n)
*
y(n) = useful signal
w(n) = random noise
z(n) = actual measured signal
real measured signal = wanted signal + noise
* Stochastic signals are more realistic but harder to analyze.

(Aliasing)
--
fs < 2 * fmax ... we get aliasing
-
aliasing means repeated spectra starts overlapping
-
when that happens, different frequneces start overlapping
A high frequency can appear to be a completely different lower frequency
-
f = 30hz
fs =  50hz
f-alias = I 30 - 50 I  = 20hz 
-
So a \(30\) Hz signal may incorrectly appear as \(20\) Hz after sampling.
Once aliasing has happened, the original information can be impossible to recover

(spatial frequency)
For audio:
$$ \text{frequency}=\text{how fast the signal changes over time} $$
For images:
$$ \boxed{\text{spatial frequency}=\text{how fast pixel values change over space}} $$
Imagine black and white stripes.
-
Imagine black and white stripes.
Wide stripes:
$$ \text{slow changes} \rightarrow \text{low spatial frequency} $$
Very thin stripes:
$$ \text{rapid changes} \rightarrow \text{high spatial frequency} $$
